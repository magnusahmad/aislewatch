AisleWatch

A 4B vision model that watches containment doors better than frontier APIs, at a fraction of the cost.

Every 5 seconds, ten CCTV frames go in and a structured caption comes out: is the cold-aisle door closed, ajar, propped open, or is someone passing through? Below: how the labels were made, the untrained model, the trained one, and how it compares.

Scores are on camera B, which no model trained on: 114 windows from 18 scenes. All footage on this page is simulated.
Step 1

A frontier model labels the training data

Gemini 3.8 Flash watches each 5-second window from the training cameras (six of the seven rooms are shown) and writes the caption field by field. A person reviews every label before it's used for training.

Playing at real speed: 2 frames per second
Step 2

Before: Qwen3.5-4B out of the box

The same open model with no training, on camera B. It never trained on this camera. Each field is graded against the human label: ✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the correct value struck alongside.

Same 8 windows in steps 2 and 3: five types of error, two easy cases, and one that both models miss.
Step 3

After: AisleWatch, fine-tuned on the reviewed labels

The same 4B model with a LoRA adapter trained on 713 labelled windows from seven rooms, in one epoch on one GPU (65 minutes, $0.99). Same windows, same grading.

Camera B was held out of training throughout.
Step 4

Accuracy, cost and speed against frontier models

All models get the same 114 camera-B windows and the same prompt. Cost assumes continuous monitoring: one 5-second window after another, 518,400 windows per camera per month.
